bump: major
section: Breaking changes
---
- **The frame draws the platform the device runs**: the status bar used to be an iOS one on every device, and the bottom inset an iOS home indicator. An Android device now gets Android's own status bar — the clock hard against the left edge, and the signal, Wi-Fi and battery in Android's order rather than iOS's — and Android's navigation instead of the home indicator. iOS and a device that states no `platform` keep their own shape.
- **`AndroidNavigation`, and `Device.pixel9ProXL()`'s corrected insets**: an Android device states whether it shows gesture navigation or the three-button bar, which the frame draws and which sets how much of the screen the system keeps. Gesture navigation reserves 24.0 and the three-button bar 48.0; `Device.pixel9ProXL()` defaults to gestures, Android's own default. Its status bar is 50.3, measured, where it used to say 48, and its bottom inset used to say 40, which is neither mode. **Goldens of `Device.pixel9ProXL()`, or of any Android device of your own that follows these numbers, will move and need regenerating** — the window the widget is laid out in is 18 logical pixels taller than it was. `Device.pixel9ProXL(navigation: AndroidNavigation.threeButton)` is named apart from the gesture one, so a test can run both and file a golden under each.
- **The Pixel reports its punch-hole camera**: `Device.pixel9ProXL()` now carries the cutout Android reports for it, which the frame draws as a lens and which reaches the widget under test in `MediaQuery.displayFeatures`, as it does on a device. Flutter lays dialogs, popups and other `DisplayFeatureSubScreen` routes out around a reported cutout, so **a golden of a dialog on the Pixel will move**, down below the status bar, which is where one sits on the device. The bounds are a Pixel 6a's, scaled to this device's width.
- **Nothing fills a bar any more, so every golden with a status bar moves**: the frame used to paint the status bar band with the window's own background, white or black. A device does not. The status bar and the navigation are drawn over the app, which is why a `Scaffold`'s app bar colour runs to the top edge of a real screen with the clock sitting on it — the fill hid exactly that, and showed a strip no device displays. The bands are now left to the app and only the glyphs are drawn. **Every golden whose device reports a top inset will move and needs regenerating**, whatever its platform.
- **The navigation is drawn because the mode says so, not because an inset is non-zero**: a window that reports no bottom inset still shows the navigation its device is set to, and one that reports more than the mode needs leaves the extra space above the bar. `AndroidNavigation.none` draws nothing at all, for a golden of a window drawn edge to edge.
- **`goldenTestShowStatusBar` and `goldenTestShowNavigation`**: two switches for `flutter_test_config.dart` that stop the frame drawing either bar across a whole suite. Both leave the device's insets alone, so the app lays out exactly as it did; only the drawing stops.
