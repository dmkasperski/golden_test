bump: major
section: Breaking changes
---
- **The frame draws the platform the device runs**: the status bar used to be an iOS one on every device, and the bottom inset an iOS home indicator. An Android device now gets Android's own status bar — the clock hard against the left edge, and the signal, Wi-Fi and battery in Android's order rather than iOS's — and Android's navigation instead of the home indicator. iOS and a device that states no `platform` are drawn exactly as before, so only Android goldens move.
- **`AndroidNavigation`, and `Device.pixel9ProXL()`'s corrected insets**: an Android device states whether it shows gesture navigation or the three-button bar, which the frame draws and which sets how much of the screen the system keeps. Gesture navigation reserves 24.0 and the three-button bar 48.0; `Device.pixel9ProXL()` defaults to gestures, Android's own default. Its status bar is 50.3, measured, where it used to say 48, and its bottom inset used to say 40, which is neither mode. **Goldens of `Device.pixel9ProXL()`, or of any Android device of your own that follows these numbers, will move and need regenerating** — the window the widget is laid out in is 18 logical pixels taller than it was. `Device.pixel9ProXL(navigation: AndroidNavigation.threeButton)` is named apart from the gesture one, so a test can run both and file a golden under each.
- **The Pixel reports its punch-hole camera**: `Device.pixel9ProXL()` now carries the cutout Android reports for it, which the frame draws as a lens and which reaches the widget under test in `MediaQuery.displayFeatures`, as it does on a device. Flutter lays dialogs, popups and other `DisplayFeatureSubScreen` routes out around a reported cutout, so **a golden of a dialog on the Pixel will move**, down below the status bar, which is where one sits on the device. The bounds are a Pixel 6a's, scaled to this device's width; translucent system bars are a separate Android overlay and are not drawn yet.
